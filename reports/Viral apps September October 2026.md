# One agent owned a thin viral window

Between 2026-08-14 and 2026-10-14, only **one consumer app launched inside the window with cited evidence of both real users and revenue: Meta's Muse**, a personal AI agent launched Sept 8 that hit No. 1 free on the US App Store around Sept 18. Two near-misses add texture: **xAI's Grok Bot** has both metrics but launched Aug 11, three days early, and **Boothie**, an indie AI selfie app, expanded to Google Play in September with RevenueCat-verified revenue but a June iOS launch. Read every number below with three caveats. The evidence for this window is thin. Every figure came from search-engine snippets, because the research proxy blocked full-page fetches. Nearly all revenue figures are third-party estimates (Appfigures, Sensor Tower, Apptopia), not company disclosures, and the trackers disagree by about 2x on the same day. The useful pattern for Aria is not "build an agent". It is that distribution produced downloads while a paywall produced revenue, and the two rarely came from the same move.

## Muse proves reach buys installs, not money

Muse had the window's strongest user evidence. Estimates run from about **1.1M devices in 11 days** ([Appfigures](https://appfigures.com/resources/insights/meta-finally-gets-serious-about-ai-muse-million-downloads)) to **more than 5M downloads by Sept 30**, which Sensor Tower says took 22 days against 56 for ChatGPT ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/maryroeloffs/2026/09/30/metas-muse-ai-assistant-hits-5-million-downloads-outpacing-growth-of-chatg)). Sensor Tower and Similarweb put daily users near **2M by early October** ([Advisor Perspectives](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/articles/2026/10/12/metas-muse-is-ending-its-honeymoon-phase-on-solid-ground)). Revenue was tiny. Appfigures estimated about **$27K net in the first 11 days, roughly 2.5 cents per download**, though one snippet attributed $175K and $1M figures to Muse, which looks like a mix-up with Grok Bot and should be treated as unresolved. Muse also sells on web and WhatsApp, so store-only estimates understate it. Appfigures' CEO called the installs "curiosity rather than demand", driven by Meta cross-promoting through its own apps, and Apptopia data points to downloads plateauing by mid-October. A short-seller-linked source reports daily installs down about 21% from peak, which is a biased source.

## Paywalls monetized faster than free-first launches

Grok Bot earned about **$175K net in its first 11 days**, roughly 6x Muse, on far fewer installs (about 1M by Sept 19, and 400K+ weekly users a month in), because it launched into existing paid SuperGrok bundles ([Appfigures](https://appfigures.com/resources/insights/meta-finally-gets-serious-about-ai-muse-million-downloads)). Boothie, a retro-style AI selfie app, shows the indie version: **$44,405 in the last 30 days, RevenueCat-verified on TrustMRR**, though the snapshot date is unclear and user counts were not found. The indie notes otherwise found no consumer app that both broke out in the window and carries verified revenue, which likely reflects thin indexing of very recent posts rather than a true absence.

## Gauth is the one study app with both metrics

Gauth (ByteDance), an AI homework helper and not a new launch, rode back-to-school to **#3 in US Education in September 2026**. ByteDance claimed **200M+ downloads** in an Aug 31 press release, alongside new AI math courses. A third-party estimate puts it at about **$4M a month on iOS**, the #2 Education earner for September, on a $9.99 to $11.99 monthly freemium plan. No focus, app-blocking, ADHD or habit app showed a dated spike in the window. Brick, a phone-blocking device, got back-to-school press, but its only sales signal is an undated Amazon "bought in past month" badge. Social, dating and campus apps produced funding news (Rivet, a dating app, raised a $10.5M seed and launched Sept 24) but no app with both metrics. Muse is also already fading, with daily new users down about 62% from mid-September per TickerTrends data reported by Hunterbrook.

## Apps with only one metric do not prove a business

Several apps show users but no revenue in the window. Vinted hit No. 2 US free on Oct 4, but its US growth started in early 2026. Grok's main app grew US downloads 16.6% to 1.9M in August. Claude's August downloads fell 10.9% to 3.2M. Instinct, an SMS-only AI agent described as viral, raised a $1B Series C at a $10B valuation on Sept 28 with no user numbers shared ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/28/viral-ai-agent-instinct-raises-1)). No AI photo or video trend in the window could be tied to measurable downloads, unlike Nano Banana in Sept 2025. Appfigures found in May 2026 that image-model launches drive 6.5x more downloads than chatbot updates but do not reliably convert to revenue. Category money is real elsewhere: Sensor Tower projected AI in-app revenue above **$4B in H1 2026**, and companion apps took **$162.8M** in H1 across 214 apps.

## Three patterns explain the window

First, distribution beat novelty. Muse won the chart because Meta owns the feed, not because users went looking. A solo founder cannot buy that, so organic hooks matter more. Second, a paid habit beats a free spike. Grok Bot and Boothie earned more per user by charging early. Third, the window's energy was "agents that act for you", while filters went quiet. For a 13+ procrastination app, the transferable idea is an app that does a visible piece of work for the user, priced from day one.

## App ideas grounded in the evidence

**1. Ampora "Done-for-you first move."** Target: students who freeze at a blank assignment. Hook: paste a rubric, get the first 10 minutes pre-built and a lock that starts on tap. Monetization: free trial then subscription. Why now: Muse made "agent does it for me" mainstream, and Ampora already has the breakdown pipeline.

**2. Homework-deadline agent by text.** Target: high schoolers who ignore apps. Hook: text a photo of a syllabus, get scheduled nudges by SMS, Instinct-style. Monetization: monthly plan billed to a parent. Why now: Instinct shows SMS-only agents can go viral without an app.

**3. Study-session photo booth.** Target: 13 to 18 students on TikTok. Hook: finish a locked session, get a Boothie-style retro "proof" card to share. Monetization: premium styles. Why now: Boothie shows selfie restyling still earns $44K a month at indie scale.

**4. Parent-funded focus plan.** Target: parents of ADHD teens. Hook: weekly report of sessions served, with no surveillance of content. Monetization: family subscription. Why now: paywalled launches into a payer monetize about 6x faster than free ones.

**5. Exam-week sprint pass.** Target: students before finals. Hook: a two-week auto-schedule plus lock bundle. Monetization: one-time purchase. Why now: curiosity spikes fade within weeks, so sell the spike.

**6. Body-double companion.** Target: ADHD adults and older teens. Hook: a calm AI voice that starts the session with you. Monetization: subscription. Why now: companion apps earned $162.8M in H1 2026.

## Conclusion

The window rewards a paywall attached to a visible result, not a free download spike. **Best recommendation: build idea 1 inside Ampora.** Make the First move feel like an agent finished the hardest ten minutes for you, then charge from the first session.
